1Interpretation

  1. The following definitions apply in this Agreement:
    Agreed Reference Rate
    the reference rate against which committed pricing is set for a Corridor, as specified in the relevant Trade Confirmation and determined when a payment is accepted.
    Applicable Laws
    all statutes, regulations, rules, by-laws, subordinate legislation, binding codes of practice, and judgments, sanctions, directives, orders or requirements of any regulatory authority (including regulatory permits and licences), in each case to the extent applicable to a Party's performance under this Agreement and in force from time to time during the Term.
    Business Day
    a day, other than a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday, on which commercial banks are open for general business in the principal places of business of both Parties.
    Calculation Agent
    t-0, acting in the role described in Clause 7.7.
    Committed Basis
    dealing on the Network under one or more Trade Confirmations, as described in Clause 7.
    Committed Flow
    payment flow that a Requestor has committed to route, or that t-0 has committed to a Provider, under a Trade Confirmation.
    Committed Margin
    the margin over the Agreed Reference Rate at which committed pricing is offered, as set out in the relevant Trade Confirmation.
    Committed Rate
    the Committed Margin plus the Agreed Reference Rate, as set out in the relevant Trade Confirmation.
    Committed Volume
    the volume of in-scope flow, in a specified Corridor and period, that a Requestor commits to route under a Requestor Trade Confirmation.
    Confidential Information
    all information in any form which concerns the Disclosing Party and is expressed to be confidential or would reasonably be regarded as confidential, including information relating to the Services, the Disclosing Party's business, finances, technology and know-how, and the terms and negotiation of this Agreement; excluding information that was lawfully known to the Receiving Party before receipt, was received from a third party with the right to disclose it, is or becomes public without the Receiving Party's fault, or is independently developed by the Receiving Party.
    Corridor
    has the meaning given in Schedule 1.
    Data Protection Legislation
    all laws and regulations relating to the processing, protection and privacy of personal data that apply to a Party from time to time in the jurisdictions in which it operates, in each case as amended, replaced or superseded.
    Deficit Fee
    the fee payable by t-0 to a Provider on the shortfall between realised volume and t-0's committed volume to that Provider, as set out in the relevant Provider Trade Confirmation.
    Effective Date
    the date on which the Partner signs the Order Form, which may be signed electronically through the t-0 dashboard.
    End Customer
    has the meaning given in Schedule 1.
    Fees
    the fees payable under this Agreement, including the fees set out in the Order Form and any Shortfall Fees, Minimum Access Fees, Network Fees and other amounts payable under a Trade Confirmation.
    Force Majeure Event
    has the meaning given in Clause 19.1.
    In-scope flow
    payment flow that matches every flow characteristic stated in the relevant Trade Confirmation, including direction, settlement asset, payment rail(s), payment type(s), industry or use, and any special condition; flow that does not match is out of scope and does not count toward any Committed Volume or realised volume.
    Insolvency Event
    any of the events customarily constituting insolvency, including a Party being unable to pay its debts within the meaning of section 123 of the Insolvency Act 1986, the making of an administration or winding-up order or resolution (other than a solvent reconstruction), the appointment of a receiver, administrator or similar officer, entry into an arrangement or composition with creditors, cessation of business, or any analogous event in any jurisdiction.
    Intellectual Property Rights
    patents, rights to inventions, copyright and related rights, trademarks, business and domain names, goodwill, rights in designs, database rights, rights in confidential information (including know-how and trade secrets) and all other intellectual property rights, in each case whether registered or unregistered and including all applications and rights to apply, that subsist now or in the future anywhere in the world.
    Licences
    any applicable licences, registrations, authorisations, permits, consents and approvals.
    Minimum Access Fee
    the monthly fee payable by a Requestor for access to committed rates in a Corridor where no Committed Volume is committed, as set out in the relevant Requestor Trade Confirmation.
    Network Fee
    a fee charged by t-0 on settled or In-scope volume, payable by a Provider for introduced flow as set out in the Network Fee Schedule, and by a Requestor where stated in a Trade Confirmation.
    Network Fee Schedule
    the schedule of network fees payable by Providers for introduced flow, published separately by t-0 on a per-currency basis and updated from time to time, referenced in Schedule 1 and subject to variation for a specific flow by a Trade Confirmation.
    Non-executed Rate
    a day on which, as verified by t-0 as Calculation Agent, the rate quoted through the Network for an In-scope payment was worse than the Committed Rate, with the effect set out in the relevant Trade Confirmation.
    Order Form
    the order form signed by the Partner and t-0.
    Partner's Manager / t-0's Manager
    the individuals identified as such on the Order Form or as otherwise notified from time to time.
    Payment Rules
    the rules set out in Schedule 1 (Payment Rules, the Network Operating Manual), as amended under Clause 21.
    Provider
    a licensed financial institution that executes payments locally on the Network, acting as a Payout Provider or a Collection Provider (as defined in Section 2 of Schedule 1). Committed rates are published under a Provider Trade Confirmation.

    a Partner that originates payment activity and holds the End Customer relationship, acting as a Payout Requestor or a Collection Requestor (as defined in Section 2 of Schedule 1).     the fee payable by a Requestor to t-0 on the shortfall between its Committed Volume and its realised volume in a Corridor, as set out in the relevant Requestor Trade Confirmation.
    Supported Blockchains
    the blockchains supported by the t-0 Network for settlement in the Settlement Currency from time to time.
    t-0 Network
    has the meaning given in Schedule 1.
    Term
    the period from the Effective Date until this Agreement is terminated.
    Trade Confirmation
    a document (however executed, including electronically or through the Network) agreed between t-0 and a Partner setting out binding committed terms for Committed Flow, as described in Clause 7.

7Committed flow and Trade Confirmations

  1. Basis of dealing. A Partner may transact on the Network on an uncommitted basis, using standing, non-binding indicative terms recorded in the Order Form and adjusted through the API, or, where agreed with t-0, on a Committed Basis under one or more Trade Confirmations.
  2. Trade Confirmations. A Trade Confirmation sets out binding committed terms for Committed Flow. On execution, each Trade Confirmation forms part of this Agreement and is subject to it, save as provided in the order of precedence in Clause 3. Trade Confirmations may be executed in writing, by electronic signature, or through the Network.
  3. Requestor Trade Confirmations. A Requestor will enter into a separate Trade Confirmation for each Corridor, specifying either (a) a Committed Volume for that Corridor and the Shortfall Fee payable if realised volume falls short, or (b) where no Committed Volume is agreed, the Minimum Access Fee payable for access to committed rates; together with the start date, any ramp period, the tenor or cancellation notice, and any Security Deposit.
  4. Provider Trade Confirmations. A Provider will enter into a single Trade Confirmation with t-0, incorporating a rate schedule that sets out, for each Corridor the Provider serves, the Committed Rate (the Committed Margin over the Agreed Reference Rate), the Deficit Fee, the settlement service level and any force majeure terms. Corridors may be added by amending the rate schedule.
  5. t-0 as counterparty. For Committed Flow, t-0 acts as principal counterparty: it receives Committed Volume commitments from Requestors and gives corresponding volume commitments to Providers, and is entitled to receive and liable to pay the associated Fees.
  6. In-scope flow and Non-executed Rates. Committed Volumes and Fees relate to In-scope flow only. The Requestor warrants that it will submit only In-scope flow and will not mischaracterise any payment. A Trade Confirmation may provide that, where a rate quoted through the Network is worse than the Committed Rate, the affected day is a Non-executed Rate that reduces the Committed Volume and relieves the corresponding Fees, and may allow termination, as that Trade Confirmation states.
  7. Settlement unaffected. t-0's role as principal under Clause 7.5 relates only to the volume and rate commitments and associated Fees under Trade Confirmations. The processing and settlement of each individual payment continues to be governed by the Payment Rules in Schedule 1, under which t-0 provides facilitating software, holds no Partner funds, and routes messages between Partners. t-0 is not a counterparty to, and accepts no liability for, the underlying payments themselves.
  8. Calculation Agent. t-0 acts as Calculation Agent for all Committed Flow. Using the secure ledger it maintains and its verification of on-chain settlement, t-0 will determine realised In-scope volume, any Non-executed Rate days, any shortfall or deficit, settlement timing and any Fee payable. Absent manifest error, its determinations are binding on the Parties at first instance, without prejudice to Clause 30 (Dispute resolution).
  9. Force majeure. Relief for events outside a Party's reasonable control is dealt with in Clause 19 and, where more specific to a Committed Flow, in the relevant Trade Confirmation.

8Fees, Security Deposits and set-off

  1. The Partner will pay the Fees set out in the Order Form, which accrue and are payable in accordance with its terms.
  2. In addition, the Partner will pay any Shortfall Fees, Minimum Access Fees and Network Fees, and t-0 will pay any Deficit Fees, in each case as set out in the relevant Trade Confirmation and as determined by the Calculation Agent.
  3. Network Fee. t-0 charges a Network Fee on settled or In-scope volume. A Provider pays the Network Fee for introduced flow as set out in the Network Fee Schedule. t-0 may also charge a Requestor a Network Fee where stated in a Trade Confirmation. A Network Fee may be varied for a specific flow by a Trade Confirmation.
  4. Minimum Access Fee. The Minimum Access Fee for a Corridor is a monthly floor. In any month, the Requestor pays only the amount (if any) by which the Minimum Access Fee exceeds the Requestor's processed volume in that Corridor multiplied by the Shortfall Fee percentage stated in the relevant Trade Confirmation.
  5. Security Deposits. t-0 may require a Requestor to post and maintain a Security Deposit in the Settlement Currency in a wallet designated by t-0, as security for the Requestor's obligations under any Trade Confirmation. t-0 may apply or draw down the Security Deposit against any Fees (including Shortfall Fees) properly due and unpaid. Any balance remaining after all amounts due have been satisfied will be returned on termination of the relevant Trade Confirmation.
  6. Set-off. t-0 may set off any amount owed by a Partner under this Agreement against any amount owed by t-0 to that Partner (including Deficit Fees), whether under the same or a different Trade Confirmation. Except as expressly provided, all amounts are payable without set-off or deduction by the Partner.
  7. t-0 will provide an invoice on request for Fees paid via the t-0 Network during the Partner's specified accounting period.
  8. Unless otherwise stated, all Fees are exclusive of VAT and other applicable taxes, which the Partner will pay in addition.

16Non-circumvention and non-solicitation

  1. During the Term and for one year after termination or expiry (the Restricted Period), the Partner will not, directly or indirectly, whether on its own behalf or with or for any third party: (a) engage in any arrangement for executing cross-border payments with any other participant in the t-0 Network (or their affiliates) with whom it has previously transacted, or into whose Committed Flow it has been matched or aggregated, via the t-0 Network (each a Restricted Partner); or (b) circumvent or seek to circumvent the t-0 Network for the purpose of executing cross-border payments with a Restricted Partner.
  2. During the Restricted Period, the Partner will not, directly or indirectly, solicit, entice or encourage any Restricted Partner to enter into any cross-border payments arrangement outside the t-0 Network, or induce any Restricted Partner to reduce or terminate its involvement with the t-0 Network.
  3. Where a Restricted Partner has been introduced or aggregated through Committed Flow, the Restricted Period runs from the later of termination of this Agreement and the last date on which the Parties participated in Committed Flow involving that Restricted Partner.
  4. The restrictions in this Clause 16 do not apply where the Partner can provide documentary evidence that its relationship with the relevant counterparty existed independently and before any introduction via the t-0 Network.
  5. Nothing in this Clause limits t-0's right to seek injunctive or other equitable relief for an actual or threatened breach, in addition to damages or other remedies.

17Suspension

  1. t-0 may suspend the Partner's access to the Services, in whole or in part (including by removing the Partner's committed capacity from the aggregate position in a Corridor), immediately on notice, where: (a) the Partner is in material breach of this Agreement; (b) the Partner has failed to pay any amount properly due and that failure continues; (c) t-0 reasonably suspects financial crime, a sanctions concern, or a breach of Applicable Law; (d) the Partner is subject to, or t-0 reasonably considers it likely to become subject to, an Insolvency Event; or (e) t-0 reasonably considers suspension necessary to protect the integrity or security of the t-0 Network or other Partners.
  2. Suspension does not relieve the Partner of obligations accrued before or during the suspension. t-0 will restore access promptly once the cause of suspension has been resolved to its reasonable satisfaction, and will act proportionately, limiting any suspension to what is reasonably necessary.

18Termination

  1. Either Party may terminate this Agreement on not less than three months' prior written notice, provided the notice does not expire before the end of the initial term set out in the Order Form (the Initial Term).
  2. If the Partner continues to use the Services after a renewal date, it is deemed to have renewed for the following term.
  3. Without prejudice to other rights, either Party may terminate immediately on written notice if the other: (a) commits a material breach that, if capable of remedy, is not remedied within 30 days of written notice; (b) commits persistent or continuous service-level failures, including persistent failures to meet the notification and execution Service Levels in Sections 5 and 10 of Schedule 1; (c) is subject to an Insolvency Event; or (d) ceases to trade.
  4. t-0 may terminate immediately if the Partner commits, is prosecuted for, or is found to have committed, any breach of Applicable Law or any Licence, or is in breach of Schedule 1.
  5. Individual Trade Confirmations may be terminated in accordance with their own terms without terminating this Agreement. Termination of this Agreement terminates all Trade Confirmations then in effect.
  6. On termination, this Agreement ceases to have effect except for Clauses 8 (in respect of accrued amounts), 11, 14, 15, 16, 19 to 33, which survive. Termination does not affect rights or obligations accrued at the date of termination.

19Force majeure

  1. A Force Majeure Event is any event beyond a Party's reasonable control and not caused by its own act, omission or breach, including: an outage or degradation of the t-0 Network; a halt, severe congestion or finality failure of a settlement blockchain; downtime of a local payment rail; a government-imposed payment moratorium, capital control or currency inconvertibility; a change of law, licence or regulatory direction that prevents performance in a Corridor; a sanctions designation arising after the relevant Trade Confirmation; a security incident not attributable to the affected Party's own controls; and natural disaster, war, civil unrest, terrorism or pandemic, in each case to the extent it actually prevents performance.
  2. Neither Party is liable for any failure or delay in performance to the extent caused by a Force Majeure Event. The affected obligations are suspended for the duration of the event. The affected Party will notify the other promptly, use reasonable endeavours to mitigate, and resume performance as soon as practicable.
  3. For Committed Flow, relief is proportionate to the impairment: no Shortfall Fee or Deficit Fee accrues on flow actually prevented by the Force Majeure Event, and any Committed Volume is reduced for the days affected, as elaborated in the relevant Trade Confirmation. The Calculation Agent will determine, binding at first instance, whether a Force Majeure Event occurred and the extent of the impairment.
  4. If a Force Majeure Event continues for more than 30 days, either Party may terminate the affected Trade Confirmation or Corridor on notice.

Payment Rules — Network Operating Manual

1Introduction and Purpose

1.1Status. This Schedule 1 forms part of the Agreement and sets out the binding operational rules that every Network Member must observe. Capitalised terms have the meanings given in the Agreement or in Section 23 (Definitions). t-0 may amend this Schedule in accordance with Clause 21 of the Agreement.

1.2Scope. These rules apply to all Members, in whichever of the Requestor and Provider roles they operate, and cover the full operational lifecycle: onboarding and due diligence, quoting, payment processing, settlement, compliance, fraud, refunds and disputes. Settlement between Members occurs exclusively in USDT on the Supported Blockchains.

1.3Infrastructure neutrality. In processing and settling individual payments, t-0 operates purely as technical infrastructure: it does not touch the flow of funds of payments and takes no custody of any Member wallet, other than any Security Deposit held under Clause 8 of the Agreement. This neutrality is subject to, and does not limit, t-0's role as principal counterparty for Committed Flow and as Calculation Agent under Clause 7 of the Agreement.

1.4Governing principles. The Network operates on the following principles:

  1. No custody: each Member retains full custody and control of its own wallets and funds; the Network monitors settlement transactions but never holds or controls Member wallets, save for any Security Deposit held under the Agreement.
  2. Bilateral trust: corridors are established through mutual KYB approval, and payments are never routed to a counterparty the sending Member has not pre-approved.
  3. Regulatory responsibility: each Member is independently responsible for compliance with all laws and licensing requirements in its own operating jurisdictions.

2Roles

2.1Requestors and Providers. Every Member operates as a Requestor, a Provider, or both. A Requestor originates payment activity and holds the relationship with the End Customer; it requests a service from the Network. A Provider is the institution that executes that service locally. The Provider role carries the greater operational burden, because the Provider takes responsibility for receiving and acting on introduced flow. The Requestor role is intentionally light-touch.

2.2There are four role types, formed by the two directions of flow (Payout and Collection):

RoleWhat it does
Payout RequestorOriginates outbound payments and instructs a Payout Provider to disburse local currency to a beneficiary. Settles USDT to the Provider.
Payout ProviderA licensed institution that receives payout requests and disburses local currency to the beneficiary via domestic rails, in exchange for USDT settlement.
Collection RequestorOriginates collections by creating payment intents to collect fiat from a payer in a market, and receives USDT settlement.
Collection ProviderA licensed institution that collects local currency from the payer, confirms receipt, and settles the corresponding USDT to the Collection Requestor.

2.3Multiple roles. A single Member may hold more than one role (for example, a Payout Requestor in one corridor and a Payout Provider in another). All roles are accessed through the same API integration.

2.4Single-leg transactions. Each Network transaction is a single leg between one Requestor and one Provider. The Network does not chain a Collection and a Payout into one end-to-end payment; a Member that requires both runs two separate transactions.

3Membership and Eligibility

3.1Providers. Only licensed financial institutions may act as Providers. A Provider must hold and maintain all Licences required to execute local-currency payouts or collections in its market, and must evidence this during onboarding.

3.2Requestors. A Requestor is not required to hold a financial-services licence. Every Requestor must nonetheless complete the Network standard KYB and due diligence with t-0 (Section 13), which Providers review before agreeing to execute its payment requests, and warrants on a continuing basis that it complies with all laws applicable to it, including anti-money-laundering, counter-terrorist-financing and sanctions laws, and that it performs due diligence on its own End Customers.

3.3Onboarding. All prospective Members complete a pre-approval KYB and due diligence process through the t-0 Platform before being granted access, submitting company, ownership, regulatory and (for Providers) licensing information.

3.4Corridor establishment. Before two Members exchange payments they must establish a corridor. Corridor approval requires mutual KYB review and explicit approval by each Member's compliance function through the t-0 dashboard. t-0 facilitates the introduction and documentation exchange but the approval decision rests solely with each Member. No payment is routed through a corridor that has not been bilaterally approved.

4Requestor Operating Obligations

The Requestor role is light-touch. A Requestor's operational duties are limited to originating clean, well-documented requests, meeting its compliance duties toward its own End Customers, and funding or receiving settlement. Its obligations are:

4.1Common to all Requestors.

  1. Complete and maintain Network KYB and due diligence with t-0, and keep its profile current (Section 13).
  2. Perform KYC on its End Customers and attach complete Travel Rule data to every request (Section 12).
  3. Where a Provider requires it in a corridor, complete the Provider's End Customer KYB and obtain approval before sending requests for that End Customer (Section 14).
  4. Validate the network quote against its own benchmark before confirming, and confirm within the quote validity window.
  5. Warrant compliance with all applicable AML, sanctions and local laws, and notify t-0 and the counterparty of any relevant compliance event.
  6. Retain records of its requests, Travel Rule data and settlements for audit and regulatory purposes.

4.2Payout Requestor.

  1. Originate payout requests with accurate beneficiary details and settle USDT to the Payout Provider under the agreed settlement model (Section 11).
  2. Manage its own conversion of local currency to USDT and maintain adequate USDT balances or agreed credit.
  3. Communicate payout outcomes to its End Customer.

4.3Collection Requestor.

  1. Create payment intents specifying the currency, amount and Travel Rule data, and present the returned payment options to the payer.
  2. Rely on the Network's settlement notification, not the payer's claim of payment, before releasing goods or services.
  3. Manage credit exposure to its Collection Providers within agreed limits, since the Provider holds collected funds until USDT settlement.

5Provider Operating Obligations

The Provider role requires high operational standards, compliance standards and service levels. A Provider's obligations are:

5.1Common to all Providers.

  1. Hold and maintain all required Licences and complete and maintain Network KYB and due diligence with t-0.
  2. Stream live quotes to the Network and maintain uninterrupted quote streaming during operating hours (Section 7).
  3. Screen all inbound Travel Rule data against applicable sanctions lists and perform transaction monitoring under its own compliance procedures.
  4. Review the Requestor's Network KYB and due diligence, and where operated in the corridor, define and apply its own End Customer KYB requirements (Section 14) and run manual AML checks where required (Section 15).
  5. Meet the applicable Service Level Agreements (Section 10) and manage USDT and local-currency liquidity to ensure continuous performance.
  6. Maintain its designated settlement wallet in operational condition with appropriate custody and security.
  7. Reject any request that does not meet its AML/CFT risk standards, communicating the rejection to the Network immediately.

5.2Payout Provider.

  1. Accept or reject a payout request within 30 seconds of receipt.
  2. On acceptance, the settlement amount is locked; submit the payout to the requested local payment rail within one hour of accepting the transaction.
  3. Report payout completion to the Network with receipt details and transaction identifiers.
  4. Prevent duplicate execution of the same instruction.

5.3Collection Provider.

  1. Respond to a payment intent with payment details within 30 seconds.
  2. Receive and verify the payer's fiat payment and confirm funds received to the Network promptly; the settlement rate is fixed at confirmation.
  3. Settle the corresponding USDT to the Collection Requestor within one hour of receipt of the local currency, or under the agreed settlement model (Section 11).
  4. Monitor for incoming payments against the reference details it provided.

6t-0 Responsibilities

6.1Maintain the centralised order book of FX quotes and route each request to the best available quote, considering rate and settlement-limit capacity.

6.2Monitor the Supported Blockchains for settlement transactions between whitelisted wallets and maintain the internal ledger of obligations, settlements, net positions and fee accruals.

6.3Provide real-time payment status tracking and status callbacks to all parties.

6.4Facilitate KYB and due diligence exchange between prospective corridor partners and host the End Customer KYB form builder.

6.5Act as Calculation Agent for Committed Flow under the Agreement, determining realised volume, shortfall, deficit, timing and fees.

6.6Provide SDKs, documentation, sandbox and pre-go-live verification, and integration support.

7Quote Management and FX Operations

7.1Quote streaming. Providers stream exchange-rate quotes to the Network at intervals of no less than every 5 seconds, covering the currencies, bands and directions they support. Streaming also serves as a liveness check: if a Provider stops streaming for 30 seconds, the Network treats this as downtime and removes the Provider from the order book until streaming resumes.

7.2Quote bands. Quotes are structured across standard USD-equivalent volume bands (Band 1 $1,000; Band 2 $5,000; Band 3 $10,000; Band 4 $25,000; Band 5 $250,000; Band 6 $1,000,000). Band-differentiated pricing is recommended but not mandatory.

7.3Quote validity. Quotes are valid for 30 seconds from submission. A Requestor has 30 seconds from receiving a quote to create a payment at the quoted rate.

7.4Routing. On a request, the Network converts the amount to its USD equivalent, sorts applicable quotes by rate, and considers corridor connections and settlement limits. If the best-rate Provider has insufficient limit, the Network routes to the next best quote.

7.5FX risk. Each Member manages its own exposure between local currency and USDT. Because payment can be initiated immediately against a live quote, each Member must operate a rate-validation control (human or automated) against a benchmark before confirming.

8Payment Processing — Payouts

8.1The Payout Requestor performs KYC on the sender and collects Travel Rule data.

8.2The Requestor requests a quote and confirms the payment within the 30-second validity window.

8.3The Network routes the instruction and Travel Rule data to the selected Payout Provider.

8.4The Provider screens the data and accepts or rejects within 30 seconds; on acceptance the settlement amount is locked.

8.5The Provider submits the payout to the local rail and disburses local currency to the beneficiary, then reports completion.

8.6The Network confirms payout to the Requestor and updates the ledger.

9Payment Processing — Collections

9.1The Collection Requestor creates a payment intent specifying currency, amount and Travel Rule (beneficiary) data.

9.2The Network requests payment details from available Collection Providers and returns the payment options to the Requestor.

9.3The Requestor presents the options to the payer, who sends fiat to the Provider outside the Network.

9.4On receipt, the Provider confirms funds received; the Network fixes the settlement rate from the Provider's current quote and creates ledger entries.

9.5The Network sends a settlement notification to the Requestor, after which it may safely release goods or services.

9.6The Provider settles USDT to the Requestor within one hour of receipt, or under the agreed settlement model; outstanding balances net and settle asynchronously on-chain.

11Settlement Operations

11.1Infrastructure. All inter-Member settlement occurs in USDT on the Supported Blockchains, directly between Members' whitelisted wallets, with no intermediary custody by t-0. Confirmations typically complete within 1–2 minutes.

11.2Wallets. Each Member designates a single static wallet, whitelisted with the Network for automated monitoring. Custody remains with the Member; t-0 does not access, control or check balances of Member wallets, other than any Security Deposit held under the Agreement.

11.3Settlement models — Payouts. Pre-settlement: the Requestor transfers USDT to the Provider before payout, bearing settlement risk. Post-settlement (credit-based): the Provider extends a credit line to the Requestor, enabling netting where flows are bidirectional. Hybrid: pre-settlement above an agreed credit threshold, credit-based below it.

11.4Batching and netting. Settlement is not required per payment. Members may batch obligations into a single transfer, which the Network allocates across outstanding obligations. Where credit lines exist in both directions, obligations net automatically, reducing on-chain transfers.

11.5Fees. 12.3KYC responsibilities. The Requestor is solely responsible for KYC on its End Customer. The corridor-level KYB approval between Members means the Provider is not required to KYC the Requestor's End Customers, but performs transaction-level screening on the Travel Rule data of each payment.

12.4Screening. The Provider screens all inbound Travel Rule data against applicable sanctions databases and monitors transactions under its own procedures, following the manual AML process (Section 15) where a payment is flagged, and rejecting any payment that does not meet its risk standards.

12.5Source of funds (USDT). Members acquiring USDT assume responsibility for assessing its source, using blockchain analytics and conducting due diligence on their USDT liquidity providers.

12.6Corridor-specific compliance. Travel Rule field requirements are set during corridor creation and may vary by jurisdiction. The Network passes data through as provided and does not transform or validate it.

15Manual AML Check

15.1Trigger. In place of accepting or rejecting a payment request within the response window, a Provider may respond that a manual AML check is required. The payment then enters a pending state.

15.3Quote refresh. Because the original quote may have expired during review, on approval the Network determines the Provider's current quote and recalculates the settlement amount.

15.4Last look. The Network sends the refreshed quote to the Requestor for approval. The Requestor approves or rejects: if approved, the Provider proceeds to execute at the confirmed rate; if rejected, the payment is cancelled with no settlement.

15.5Completion. On execution the Provider reports completion and the Network updates the ledger. The Provider owns the AML decision and its timing; the Requestor owns the decision to proceed at the refreshed rate.

18Security and Authentication

18.1All requests to and from the Network are cryptographically signed (equivalent to Ethereum transaction signing) and verified; modified requests fail verification and are rejected.

18.3Separate key pairs are maintained for sandbox and production; private keys are held in secure key-management systems and never hard-coded; suspected compromise is reported immediately and keys rotated.

18.4The t-0 SDK abstracts signing, timestamping and validation; application-layer authentication removes any need for IP whitelisting, though Members may add controls at their discretion.

19.2Exposure. Each Member is exposed only to its own local currency plus USDT, not to any counterparty's local currency, and should operate automated rate validation against reference rates and hedge according to its risk appetite.

1Post-transaction settlement

Where a Payout Requestor and Payout Provider establish positive Settlement Limits with each other, payouts execute immediately on instruction and settlement happens after the transaction, once a Settlement Limit is reached.

StepExample
Transaction 1Payout Requestor A (Europe) instructs Payout Provider B (Brazil) to pay 100,000 BRL (≈ $18,000) to a recipient. Limit B set against A: $1,000,000. After payout, A owes $18,000; Settlement Balance $982,000.

StepExample
